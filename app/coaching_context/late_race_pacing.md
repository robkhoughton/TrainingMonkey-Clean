# LATE-RACE PACING — WHEN HEART RATE STOPS TRACKING EFFORT

Applies to races of marathon distance or longer, and to the long runs that prepare for them.

## Two Late-Run HR Patterns — Identify Which One Before Coaching It

- **Drift up:** HR rises while pace or effort holds steady or falls. Causes: heat, dehydration, rising aerobic cost. HR is still a valid signal. Response: ease effort until HR comes back down, cool, drink.
- **Drift down / ceiling:** HR falls and will not rise when the athlete pushes, while effort feels hard. HR is no longer a valid pacing signal. Response: switch the pacing anchor (below). Never prescribe "push to get HR back into zone."
- **Rule out the sensor first.** Wrist optical HR late in a long effort can drop out or lock onto cadence, which mimics a low HR. If HR reads implausibly flat, or tracks cadence, treat it as missing data, not physiology.

## When HR Is Unreliable, Pace By

1. **Effort (RPE):** hold a steady, sustainable effort. Hike climbs when running effort spikes.
2. **Fueling cadence:** keep carbohydrate intake on its fixed time schedule. Never cut or skip fueling because HR is low — falling HR with rising effort often coincides with under-fueling.
3. **Mechanics:** keep stride frequency up with a short, quick stride and upright posture. On runnable terrain, protect cadence over stride length. Prevent the shuffle (shortened stride, low foot lift, long ground contact).

- Low HR late in a long effort does not mean spare capacity. Do not read it as room to push.
- Do not tie the switch to a fixed distance or time. The cue is the HR pattern itself; its onset is individual and moves with fitness and the day's conditions.

## Race Execution Guidance

- Early race: HR cap, easy-effort discipline, AND split discipline govern together. An HR cap and a relaxed, conversational feel do not prevent a fast start — the athlete can stay under the HR cap the whole time and still bank large time gains early, at a hidden cost in glycogen and eccentric leg damage that HR does not show.
- Give split targets per aid station or segment, derived from the goal finish plan. Through the first third of the race, the target is to arrive at each split on plan, not ahead of it. Arriving ahead of plan in the first third is a pacing error to correct, not a bonus to keep: slow down to plan, especially on descents, where HR understates the cost.
- Expect early-race HR to run higher at a given pace than in training (start-line arousal). Do not read it as poor form, and do not chase the training HR.
- Late race, once HR stops responding to effort: RPE, fueling cadence, and mechanics govern. Include this switch explicitly when giving race-day or race-week execution guidance.

## Training Application

- On long runs, have the athlete rehearse the late-race switch in the final portion: fueling on schedule, holding cadence and posture while fatigued.
- Do not prescribe extra distance to reach or extend the HR-ceiling phase.
- A long run that ended with suppressed HR and high effort does not by itself justify extra recovery days. Recovery decisions follow readiness state and load metrics.
